# Direction contract: the conservation workbench

Direction seed: `0f5180d5` from `concept-seed.mjs --scope direction --mode persuade`.
Assignment: candidate 6 of the grounded list. The round ran unattended because this implementer has no live decision channel. The assigned direction is therefore the surviving choice, not a hidden default.

## Grounded list

1. Natural-history field plate: specimen callouts make a file's named parts legible.
2. Library classification drawer: indexed slips give the Structure tree a familiar order.
3. Railroad engineering timetable: measured rows make offsets and lengths easy to scan.
4. Forensic chain-of-custody worksheet: evidence labels keep claims tied to a source span.
5. Type specimen proof: encoded bytes become a study in letterform and measurement.
6. Museum conservation worksheet: an annotated artifact, ruled measurements, and restrained material color make inspection feel careful and exact.
7. Patent drawing archive: sectional diagrams and claim callouts show relationships without pretending to decode the whole object.

## Assigned direction

**World:** The conservation workbench. HexLens is a paper-and-ink inspection desk for a digital artifact. The page uses warm conservation paper, charcoal ink, thin registration rules, and sparse oxide and verdigris accents. Display type is an editorial serif; measurements and bytes use a compact monospace.

**First viewport:** The landing page opens on one large specimen sheet. The heading sits beside a live PNG sample tracer, with the 24-byte sample strip and its three labeled Structures visible in the first frame. `Try the sample` is the signed primary action. `Open a file` is a quiet secondary action for the later local-file slice.

**Visitor path:** Read the one-line promise, see the signature and IHDR relationship, press the sample action, then select a Structure or byte span. The inspector preserves a selected-span annotation while the Field inspector explains the exact bytes.

**Signature interaction:** Selecting a Structure draws a thin oxide bracket over its Byte span and pins its Field note beside the byte row. Selecting a byte span moves the same bracket and focuses the smallest matching semantic item without moving DOM focus.

**Cross-surface reach:** The same paper, registration rules, Field notes, and accent meanings carry from landing to the desktop inspector, future WAV tracing, and the narrow Sample tabs.

**Honest risk:** Conservation language can feel precious if the interface hides the work. Keep byte values large, labels plain, and the sample visibly live in the first frame.

**Raises:**

- From the particle-detector challenger: retain concentric focus logic as a visual discipline for Selection, with one active span and surrounding context quieted.
- From the teletext challenger: keep a strict index rhythm for Structure labels and offsets, without phosphor colors or retro novelty.
- From the microfiche challenger: commit to a full environmental paper-and-ink field rather than floating generic panels.

## Challenger verdicts

- Pulp rocketship plate: declined. It loses audience identification and product clarity; the workbench keeps its diagonal annotation discipline out of the story.
- Warm consumer app surface: declined. It holds neither axis; the workbench keeps its generous touch-safe action sizing.
- Pickling brine calendar: declined. It holds neither axis; the workbench keeps the calendar's explicit stage labels as clear status language.
- Alien forest understory: declined. It loses clarity; the workbench keeps its rule that focus is the brightest signal.
- Particle-detector event display: competitive. It improves Selection clarity and gives the workbench concentric focus logic, but it does not beat the audience identification of conservation records.
- Ikeda datamatics: declined. It loses clarity; the workbench keeps its disciplined density and reduced-motion stillness.

## Quality bar

The finished build must feel authored, measured, and quiet enough to read. It must not collapse into a generic dashboard, a terminal, an illustration, or a stack of rounded cards. Product truth wins when the visual world conflicts with it.

## Finish contract

The build ends only after valid desktop and narrow screenshots, one detector run, the shipped finish review or its documented degraded equivalent, and `DESIGN.md` generated from the built interface. Unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished.
